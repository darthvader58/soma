## Catalog

Six historical Apollo Lunar Module positions come from [NASA NSSDCA's lunar site table](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/lunar_sites.html). The source explicitly identifies planetocentric Mean Earth/Polar Axis coordinates in the DE421 realization. The catalog preserves the published coordinates and source update date, 6 March 2017. Published digits do not establish absolute positional accuracy.

Nine named crater centers come from the [IAU/USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/SearchResults?Target=16_Moon&Feature+Type=9_Crater): Shackleton, Haworth, Cabeus, de Gerlache, Faustini, Nobile, Shoemaker, Malapert and Tycho. Each inspected feature page selected the LOLA 2011 control network. Values retain two decimal places. The [USGS coordinate explanation](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/GIS_Downloads) states that planetographic and planetocentric latitude coincide for its spherical lunar cartographic reference.

The inspected crater pages do not independently certify a precise `MOON_ME_DE421` realization. Their markers are contextual references, with that limitation retained in every record. Malapert denotes the crater, not Malapert Massif. Crater centers are not landing-region boundaries, certified safe sites or measured elevations.

The Gazetteer CSV was acquired through the official search-results download on 16 November 2026: 183,546 bytes, SHA256 `4cf364c7757faba6c11155400d68aceca745649f1978986116df6f7c256db73b`. Its download supplied no publication timestamp; the acquisition date is not a claim about when its coordinates were measured or updated.

All catalog positions initially use assumed zero elevation on a 1,737,400 m sphere. Python derives Cartesian display positions. The regional terrain-registration certificate from the delivery work does not certify this global catalog against the Cesium mesh.

## Matched lunar geometry

The study uses the pinned DE421 planetary ephemeris, `naif0012.tls`, `moon_080317.tf` and `moon_pa_de421_1900-2050.bpc`. The binary set supplies Moon-centered Sun/Earth states and the documented `MOON_ME_DE421` orientation. File sizes, source URLs and SHA256 values are in `data/de421-phase2.manifest.json`.

Readiness inspects actual SPK/PCK coverage and its intersection. Requested intervals must fit the selected sources. No terrestrial refraction or timestamp-plus-longitude solar-hour approximation is used. Future UTC beyond the leap-second kernel's publication cannot include unannounced future leap seconds.

Before 1972, UTC labels use SPICE's extended convention rather than reconstructing historical civil UTC. Interpret broad kernel coverage separately from the validity of a particular time convention or mission source.

## Reconstructed LRO

The source is [NASA PDS/NAIF's LRO SPICE archive](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/pds/data/lro-l-spice-6-v1.0/lrosp_1000/data/spk/spkinfo.txt), product `lrorg_2026074_2026166_v01.bsp`, created 3 September 2026. Its label identifies a regular definitive trajectory from NASA GSFC Flight Dynamics Facility. SOMA verifies LRO target −85, Moon center 301, native J2000 SPK segments and actual continuous coverage from **2026-03-15T00:00:00Z through 2026-06-15T00:00:00Z**.

The manifest pins 7,557,120 bytes and SHA256:

```text
5e17414e03c794e9dc29e4f4fde64d1d2d9fd106d87f26404188888495de28b1
```

Native SPICE evaluation supplies six-dimensional states; the engine transforms them to rotating `MOON_ME_DE421` axes. The browser may interpolate positions for smooth presentation within the supplied interval. Scientific readouts remain tied to stored epochs. No extrapolation, real-time tracking, LRO relay service or instrument footprint is implied. Attitude and instrument kernels would be required for a physical footprint.

## What validation establishes

Tests exercise coverage rejection, checksum tampering, native state/velocity semantics, local bases at the poles, independent published frame-vector comparison and restoration of SPICE kernel state. Sampling a smaller step changes the requested numerical study; coarse sampled extrema are not an event-location or physical accuracy guarantee.

This first live study evaluates reference-sphere geometry only. Terrain clearance, solar-disk masks, eclipses, power, usable contacts, coverage footprints and scheduling are not evaluated by this endpoint. The [existing delivery model](/science/assumptions-limits) has a different, bounded terrain/network scope and retains its own assumptions.
